Waste Management Handling Guidelines

Open a topic below to learn more about safe waste handling and disposal:

1. Sorting & Segregation

Separate different types of household waste before collection to reduce contamination and make recycling and composting easier.

How to sort waste

  • Use separate bins for wet organic waste, such as food scraps and garden waste, and dry waste, such as plastics, glass, and metals.
  • Keep wet waste in a covered container to help prevent leakage and pests.
  • Rinse and dry recyclable items before placing them in the dry waste bin.

Why sorting matters

  • Reduces contamination of recyclables, making recovery more efficient.
  • Keeps suitable organic material available for composting.
  • Minimizes the amount of waste sent for final disposal.
2. Reduce, Reuse & Recycle

Reducing what we throw away, reusing items, and recycling suitable materials all help conserve resources and reduce litter.

Reduce waste

  • Buy only what you need to avoid excess waste.
  • Choose products with minimal packaging where practical.
  • Use reusable bags and containers instead of single-use alternatives.

Reuse items

  • Repurpose clean containers and jars for storage.
  • Donate usable clothes and household items rather than throwing them away.
  • Repair items when practical to extend their useful life.

Recycle responsibly

  • Separate accepted recyclables such as plastics, glass, metals, and paper.
  • Clean and dry recyclables before storage or handover.
  • Confirm accepted materials and collection arrangements with your local collector.
3. Composting Organic Waste

Composting is the natural breakdown of suitable organic material into a soil-like material that can improve garden soil.

Benefits of composting

  • Reduces the amount of organic waste sent for final disposal.
  • Creates a useful soil improver for gardens.
  • Helps retain soil moisture and supports healthy soil structure.

How to start

  • Choose a shaded, well-drained spot and use a ventilated bin or covered heap.
  • Layer fruit and vegetable scraps with dry leaves or other dry plant material.
  • Keep the pile damp, not waterlogged, and turn it occasionally to let air in.
  • Avoid meat, dairy, oils, and hazardous waste in a basic home compost pile.
4. Hazardous & Medical Waste

Some household and medical items can injure people or contaminate the environment. Keep them separate from ordinary waste and use approved disposal arrangements.

Keep hazardous waste separate

  • Keep batteries, chemicals, paint, and other hazardous items out of household waste bags and community skips.
  • Do not burn, bury, or pour chemicals into drains or onto the ground.
  • Store items in their original, labelled containers where possible, away from children and heat.

Handle medical sharps safely

  • Never put loose needles, syringes, or broken medical sharps in household rubbish or recycling.
  • Do not recap, bend, or handle a used needle. If safe to do so, place it in a proper puncture-resistant sharps container.
  • Ask a health facility or local waste authority how to return or dispose of sharps safely.

Find an approved disposal option

Contact a nearby health facility or the local waste authority for current collection and drop-off instructions. Do not mix these items with general waste unless an authorised service specifically directs you to do so.

5. Using Community Skips

Community skips are designated collection containers for household waste. Use the skip assigned to your area and follow any posted instructions.

Guidelines for using community skips

  • Place household waste inside the skip in tied bags; do not leave it on the ground nearby.
  • Do not put hazardous, medical, or burning waste in a community skip.
  • Do not overfill the skip, and follow posted rules and collection guidance.
